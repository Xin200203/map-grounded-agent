## 2. Related Work

**Zero-shot goal navigation.** Frontier-based semantic exploration underpins most zero-shot systems: SemExp and PONI established learned semantic/potential scoring; CoW brought open-vocabulary detection; ESC, L3MVN, and VLFM inject language-model or vision-language priors into frontier selection; SG-Nav and UniGoal reason over incrementally built scene graphs, with UniGoal unifying object-, image- and text-goal navigation in one zero-shot framework. We adopt UniGoal as a frozen backbone precisely because it is the strongest published text-goal system (SR 20.2 on HM3D), and our question is orthogonal to all of the above: not *which prior* to inject, but *which control machinery around the prior actually earns its complexity*.

**LLM-guided navigation controllers.** Beyond frontier scoring, recent systems add planning/monitoring superstructure: SayNav and SayPlan hierarchically decompose instructions; LFG uses LLM scoring with backtracking; NavGPT and MapGPT run LLMs in the decision loop. These works report end-task gains of their full stacks; component-level attribution is typically limited to 1–3 coarse ablations on ≤15-episode suites — a regime in which, as we show (§4.1), even the sign of an SR difference is unstable. Our contribution is the missing measurement layer: matched-backbone, episode-paired, dual-LLM-strength ablation of *every* mechanism, with trace-level attribution.

**Evaluation methodology in embodied AI.** Benchmark-reliability critiques exist for embodied QA and VLN (e.g., analyses of dataset bias and metric artifacts), and Habitat challenge reports document large train/val variance. For LLM-navigation controllers specifically we are not aware of prior work quantifying minimal detectable effects, channel sensitivity of ablation conclusions, or per-mechanism binding rates. Our failure-attribution pyramid extends error-taxonomy practice (e.g., in VLN error analyses) with an oracle split that separates perception recall from semantic matching from control conversion — and, unlike post-hoc taxonomies, it is computed from replayable traces released with the system.

**Perception bottlenecks in object navigation.** Detector quality is a known limiter for modular agents; prior studies swap detectors or use ground-truth semantics to bound gains. Our root-cause result is sharper in one respect: the binding constraint in the audited system is not the detector (which fires) but the *detection→graph aggregation gate* (~6 unique captions/episode survive it); relaxing one gating parameter multiplies graph recall 2.5× and collapses the never-detected failure mode, exposing a second, controller-addressable conversion bottleneck (§4.7). This layered-bottleneck picture argues for auditing modular stacks end-to-end rather than attributing failure to "perception" wholesale.
